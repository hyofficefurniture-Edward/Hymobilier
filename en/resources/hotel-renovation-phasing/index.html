Phasing a hotel renovation: guestrooms, reception and dining

A method for splitting a hotel renovation by space — guestrooms, reception and dining — and for setting out the stages and responsibilities to confirm.

Image from the TOA Hotel & Spa Zanzibar (Tanzania) project file. A space reference, not an announcement of a delivery in France or Morocco.

Hotel renovations are rarely delivered in one phase. Splitting the work by space — guestrooms, reception, dining — keeps the property trading and lets you compare proposals on the same scope.

Why split the renovation by space

The split follows how the property operates, not how a catalogue is arranged. A guestroom wing can close while reception stays in service; the reverse is rarely possible. For each space, record downtime windows, on-site storage constraints and assembly access before you compare proposals.

Start with the guestrooms

Guestrooms carry the largest and most repetitive volume. Group them by room type and list beds, headboards, desks and storage for each type. One approved type becomes the reference for the rest; surface differences are handled afterwards, not at the point of order.

Plan the reception and public areas

Reception brings together a welcome zone, a working position and a circulation route. Counter dimensions, cable routes and accessibility requirements are reviewed against the plan, because a visually similar layout can serve a different use.

Set the restaurant and dining areas

Covers, service style and storage drive the selection. Tables, seating and buffet units are reviewed together: a table selected for the dining room will not necessarily suit the terrace or breakfast service.

Allocate responsibilities and stages

Production, transport, receiving, installation and packing removal are separate stages. Each appears in the brief with its own responsibility. That level of detail prevents two quotations from being read as the same scope.

Frequently asked questions

Which space should a hotel renovation start with?

Start with the guestrooms, because they carry the largest and most repetitive volume. One approved room type becomes the reference for the rest, which reduces adjustments later at the point of order.

Can the hotel stay open during the renovation?

Yes, provided the split follows how the property operates. A guestroom wing can close while reception stays in service. Record downtime windows, storage constraints and assembly access before comparing proposals.

Should you build a sample room before ordering?

It is advisable when several room types are involved. A sample room confirms dimensions, finishes and composition, then serves as the reference for the remaining types and for surface differences.

When should the furniture arrive on site?

Furniture should arrive when each room is ready to receive it, not earlier. Production, transport, receiving and installation are separate stages, so agree the delivery date against the works programme rather than in isolation.

Does phasing make a renovation more expensive?

It can add cost lines: on-site storage, split deliveries, protection of occupied areas and extra handling. These depend on the number of phases and site access, and belong in the itemised brief rather than a general estimate.
