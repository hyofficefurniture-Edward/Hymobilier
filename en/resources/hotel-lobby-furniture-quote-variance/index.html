Hotel lobby furniture: what moves the quote

Furniture families, customization level, invisible specifications, packaging and services: what explains the gap between two hotel lobby furniture quotes.

Atmosphere image from the group’s internal image bank, with no branding and no named project. Visual reference only, not an announcement of delivery in France or Morocco.

Two quotes prepared for lobbies of comparable size can diverge widely, and the gap is almost never explained by the manufacturer’s margin alone. It comes from what each quote understands by “lobby furniture”: which families are included, at what customization level, with which internal specifications and which services. This guide breaks those factors down so the proposals can be brought back to a comparable basis.

Why do two quotes for the same lobby diverge?

The first cause is scope. A quote covering seating, tables, the reception desk and accessories is not comparable to one limited to seating: the rest reappears later as add-on costs. The second cause is the customization level; an adjusted catalogue piece is not priced like an original drawing with a dedicated prototype and sample. The third gathers the invisible specifications — frame, cushioning, testing — that the mood image never shows. The fourth is logistics: packaging, transport and site reception can stay outside the displayed amount.

Which furniture families should the quote include?

A complete quote names each family on its own line: waiting and lounge seating, coffee and side tables, the reception desk, and accessories such as a luggage bench, space divider, planters or a coat stand. Each family carries its quantity and its control document: a workshop drawing for the reception desk, a material sheet for the seating, a packing scheme for the whole set. A quote that does not cite the document usually has not budgeted what it covers. Preparing the lobby with the same method as the hotel furniture schedule prevents omissions that turn into supplements.

Customization: catalogue, adapted or bespoke?

Three levels coexist. Catalogue adjustment keeps the existing design and changes only the fabric, lacquer or wood tone; tooling is shared across projects. Adaptation changes dimensions, adds a socket or reinforces a structure; extra preparation is added. Bespoke starts from your drawings: a prototype, a dedicated sample and committee validation before the series. The common mistake is to ask for a “distinctive style” with a catalogue budget; when the committee validates an original drawing at the third round, the prototype rework returns as a separate line if the quote excluded it.

Which invisible specifications weigh on the price?

Three layers escape the presentation image. Structure first: frame sections, material and reinforcements at the support points; intensive-use seating is designed for non-domestic usage tests. Comfort next: foam density and height, the suspension system and the resilience of the filling; high-resilience foam keeps its shape after thousands of cycles and its raw material shows in the quote. Compliance finally: the fire behaviour of the fabrics and the lot documents that allow each batch to be traced. To check this layer, require a technical sheet that cites the test, a physical sample approved against that document, and the right to inspect during production.

How to bring two quotes to a common basis?

Before placing two amounts side by side, align five points: family scope, customization level, seating specification, packing standard and delivery term. Each gap becomes a precise question: are the prototype and the sample included or billed separately, is export packing inside the price, which on-site works stay separate. The detailed method for comparing quotations on a common basis provides the framework; applied to the lobby, it turns a price gap into a list of different scopes.

Which costs appear after the signature?

Three sources recur. Packaging and transport: a figure on FOB terms covers neither the container, nor the insurance, nor the delivery to site. Installation: a lobby is fitted in a hotel in operation or in an opening on a tight schedule, with service-lift and night-work constraints. Revision rounds: every change after the approved sample reopens production, and quotes limit the number of free rounds. Ask for that number to be written in the quote, not in a later email.
