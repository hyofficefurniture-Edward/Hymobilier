PRACTICAL GUIDE

Hotel guestroom furniture: what actually changes across service tiers

Carcass, edge banding, runners, fabrics, foam, beds and hardware: how to read a hotel guestroom's tier and write it into measurable specifications.

Image from the TOA Hotel & Spa Zanzibar (Tanzania) project file. A space reference for composition and fixing details, not an announcement of a delivery in France or Morocco.

Three-star, four-star, boutique or five-star: the gap is not mainly a matter of style, but of what survives use. Two units that look alike may carry 0.4 mm or 2 mm edging, a roller runner or a full-extension runner with soft close. The markers below describe ranges seen in hotel work and are to be confirmed against supplier documentation. Here are the points that genuinely change, and how to turn them into parameters you can inspect before comparing proposals. These markers are there to frame the right questions, not to rank a supplier.

Tier shows first in the carcass

An entry-level unit often uses 16 mm particleboard with 0.4 to 0.8 mm applied edging. A higher tier moves to 18 or 19 mm board with 1.5 to 2 mm ABS edging, laid flat or flush. Band thickness matters as much as the adhesive: thin edging lifts at the first suitcase knock, and the repair shows. Behind the front, the runner decides the feel — roller, ball-bearing, then full extension with soft close. Ask for the rated load per pair, the travel and the cycle count tested. The back panel, often a thin stapled sheet, deserves the same scrutiny as the face, particularly when it carries drawers. Adjustable shelves on pins, rather than fixed battens, let a cabinet be reconfigured without tools, and that flexibility belongs in the brief. Fixings count too: wood screws in a soft board, cam fittings or inserts in a dense one. Specifying by brand name alone leaves the tier open to interpretation, so describe the assembly instead.

Fabrics, foam and removability

Abrasion resistance is read in Martindale rubs. A lightly used guestroom often holds between 20,000 and 25,000 rubs; a standard hotel room sits nearer 30,000 to 40,000; heavily used seating goes beyond 50,000. These figures are checked on the supplier's report, not on the label. Fire treatment and its classification are confirmed against the regulations of the destination country. A removable cover changes maintenance: closure type, fabric direction, washing behaviour and dimensional stability. For foam, 30 to 40 kg/m³ suits a back panel; a seat calls for a firmer density and a worked thickness, and the two should be specified separately. One spare cover per room type avoids taking a room out of service for a single stain, so state the washing regime you expect. Pile direction and shade are checked on a fitted cover, not on a sample lying flat. State whether covers are washed in-house or contracted out, because that changes the closure and the seam allowance.

The bed: frame, headboard and support

The bed concentrates the most expensive things to correct. A basic frame butts panels together; a higher tier uses solid wood or plywood legs, corner brackets, a central rail and adjustable feet. Beech or birch slats sit in holders that limit squeaking: their number and section matter more than their finish. Headboards fix in three ways — wall rail, integrated into the frame, or suspended — and a wall rail, which limits vibration, depends on the wall build-up. The mattress support, whether slats, a base or a platform, has to be described: it drives comfort and the replacement route later on. Headboard height follows the mattress top rather than the mood board: give it as a dimension from finished floor and check it against the bedside table. A close-slatted base and a sprung base are replaced differently once the floor is trading. Ask how the headboard is accessed for reupholstery without removing the bed.

Hardware, lighting and power points

Hardware decides how the room feels in use. A damped hinge with three-axis adjustment can be reset after settlement; a plain hinge only opens. Handles hold through bolted fixings or inserts: adhesive alone gives way. Mirrors integrate side or perimeter lighting, with colour temperature and colour rendering stated, and the ingress protection rating matters in the bathroom. On the electrical side, a guestroom needs sockets at the bedside, USB-A and USB-C ports, and a free point for a charger. These details weigh little at design stage and a great deal to add later, once the walls are closed. A cable route cut at machining stage avoids drilling on site and the making good that follows, so show it on the furniture drawing. Door buffers and drawer bumpers cut noise, which matters along a corridor at night. Bedside controls should be reachable from the pillow without stretching.

Storage, maintenance and the specification

Storage is drawn from use: rail height, number of adjustable shelves, internal drawers, wardrobe lighting, space for a suitcase and for a safe. A luggage rack is chosen for its height and load, not its look. A minibar needs a ventilated niche and a dedicated socket. Maintenance is prepared at order stage: removable fronts, replaceable panels, interchangeable headboard covers, identified spare parts. Five-year serviceability is judged on those possibilities, not on a promise. Finally, the specification translates tier into parameters: board and edging thickness, runner load and travel, rub count, foam density, fixing method. Those are the values that get inspected on delivery. A spares schedule with references and quantities is handed over with the room file, and that document is what keeps a floor running in year four. Safe niche dimensions and usable wardrobe depth are measured on site, not taken from the architect’s plan. Photograph the inside of a finished wardrobe; it is the part guests judge daily.
